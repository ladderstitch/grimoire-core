# Apply Confucian Life-Stage Framework

Use Confucius's six-stage life arc (Analects 2.4) as a structured lens for self-assessing developmental progress at any age, paired with the modern-psychology research that independently converges on the same milestones.

## Why This Is Best Practice

**Adopted by:** Continuously used as a self-cultivation framework across Confucian-heritage cultures for roughly 2500 years, still commonly taught and cited today. Independently, three separate 20th-century research traditions converge on the same rough decade milestones: Erikson's psychosocial stage theory, Levinson's empirical life-structure research (biographical interviews of adults, later extended across genders), and Jungian analytical psychology's account of mid-life individuation.

**Impact:** Levinson's interview-based research documented a recognizable "age-40 transition" — a period of reassessing one's life structure with more settled judgment — matching the 不惑 ("free of doubt") milestone almost exactly, 2000+ years after it was first described. Erikson's stage model remains the dominant framework taught in developmental psychology, giving the identity and generativity milestones here empirical grounding beyond the original aphorism.

**Why best:** An open-ended "how am I doing in life" reflection has no structure and no way to know what to look for. This framework gives concrete, decade-anchored checkpoints — each stage names one specific developmental task (commit, establish, de-doubt, know one's purpose and limits, accept, self-master) — turning a vague feeling into a specific, answerable self-assessment.

Sources: Confucius, *Analects* 2.4 (论语·为政); Erikson, E. (1950). *Childhood and Society*. W.W. Norton; Levinson, D. (1978). *The Seasons of a Man's Life*. Knopf; Jung, C.G., on individuation and the second half of life (collected works, 1930s-40s)

## Steps

1. **Scope the reflection.** Ask which stage(s) the user wants to work through — their current age/decade, a past one they want to revisit, or an upcoming one they want to prepare for. Don't force the full six-stage walkthrough unless they want it.

2. **Present the relevant stage(s)** — Confucian framing, the modern-psychology parallel, and concrete self-assessment questions:

   - **15 — 志于学, "set one's heart on learning"** (Erikson: identity formation beginning). Questions: What am I actively still learning right now? Have I chosen a direction, or am I drifting without one?
   - **30 — 而立, "stand firm"** (Erikson: identity vs. role confusion resolving into intimacy). Questions: Do I have an established foundation — career, relationships, principles — I could defend if challenged? Or am I still borrowing someone else's answers?
   - **40 — 不惑, "free of doubt"** (Levinson's age-40 transition: reassessing life structure with settled judgment). Questions: Am I still pulled in different directions by every competing opinion, or have I settled what I actually believe? Where do I still second-guess myself on things I've already thought through?
   - **50 — 知天命, "know the mandate of Heaven"** (Erikson: generativity vs. stagnation). Questions: Do I understand what I'm actually here to do, and what's genuinely outside my control? Am I investing in something beyond my own immediate concerns?
   - **60 — 耳顺, "the ear is obedient"** (Jungian individuation: integrating what one previously resisted, less ego-defensiveness). Questions: Can I hear direct disagreement or criticism without an inner flare of resistance? Where do I still need to be right?
   - **70 — 从心所欲，不逾矩, "follow the heart's desires without overstepping the bounds"** (Erikson: ego integrity vs. despair). Questions: Do my instincts and my principles point the same direction now, or am I still restraining impulses I no longer actually have?

3. **Get concrete answers.** Have the user actually answer the questions for their real, current stage — not rhetorically. This is a reflection tool: the output is the user's own answers, not a verdict handed to them.

4. **Name the gap plainly, without a failing grade.** Where the answers show the user hasn't reached the milestone typical of their age, say so directly, framed as a current growth edge — the stage describes ongoing work, not a deadline that was missed.

5. **Offer a next step.** If the user wants to turn an identified gap into something actionable, hand off to `design-goal-setting-framework`. If they just wanted the reflection, stop here.

## Rules

- These are traditional, descriptive milestones, not a diagnostic instrument or a timeline anyone is required to hit on schedule — never tell a user they are "failing" for not matching their decade.
- Treat the ages as illustrative anchors from a specific historical and cultural context, not literal universal deadlines — a 35-year-old who hasn't reached 而立 yet isn't behind, just still working on it.
- Don't force all six stages on someone who only wants to think through one or two.
- Keep the self-assessment questions concrete and answerable — abstract restatement of the Confucian phrase without a question the user can actually answer isn't useful.

## Examples

**Trigger:** "I just turned 40 and feel like I should have this figured out by now — what should I even be asking myself?"
→ Present the 不惑 stage: Confucian framing (free of doubt) + Levinson's age-40 transition parallel + the two self-assessment questions. Get concrete answers. If the user identifies specific unsettled convictions, name that as this decade's actual growth edge rather than a personal failing, then offer to move to goal-setting if they want next steps.

**Trigger:** "What should I be focused on in my 50s?"
→ Present the 知天命 stage alone (user specified one decade). Cover the generativity parallel and the two questions about purpose and locus of control. Don't walk through all six stages unprompted.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the ages as hard deadlines.** Telling someone they've "failed" the 30 milestone because they're 32 and still finding their footing contradicts the framework's own spirit and this skill's explicit Rules.
- **Handing over a verdict instead of eliciting answers.** The value is in the user's own concrete self-assessment, not in the assistant declaring "you're at 不惑" based on a couple of sentences.
- **Skipping straight to advice.** Presenting the stage and immediately jumping to solutions skips the actual reflection step — get the user's answers first, then identify gaps.

## When NOT to Use

- Clinical mental-health assessment — this is a reflective framework, not a diagnostic tool; if concerns point toward depression, anxiety, trauma, or another clinical issue, refer to a mental health professional or the psychology/clinical domain instead.
- The user wants a single concrete goal-setting session with no self-reflection component — use `design-goal-setting-framework` directly.
- Highly time-pressured situations needing an immediate decision, not a developmental reflection.
